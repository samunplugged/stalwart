# Sameer's fork of Stalwart

This is Sameer Gupta's fork of the [Stalwart](https://github.com/stalwartlabs/stalwart)
mail server. It is Stalwart v1.2.1 plus a few features, one commit per
feature, so that each can be rebased onto later Stalwart releases and
offered upstream.

| Feature | Setting | Off when |
|---|---|---|
| [Organization quota](#organization-quota-quota-transfer) | `Email` → `organizationQuotaDomain` | unset (the default) |
| [Mirrored blob store](#mirrored-blob-store) | `BlobStore` with `"@type": "Mirrored"` | another blob store type is chosen (the default) |
| [Account fence](#account-fence) | `PostgreSql` data store → `accountFence` | `false` (the default) |

## Organization quota (quota transfer)

The rule: **an organization's mailbox quotas always add up to its limit.**
Stalwart's own disk quota check per account stays as it is, so while every
mailbox is within its quota the organization is within its limit, and no
organization counter is needed. When a write would take an account over its
quota and the account is a member of an **organization group**, unused
quota is moved to it from the group member with the most free space.

### Setting

`organizationQuotaDomain` on the `Email` settings object (WebUI: Email →
Storage → Organization quota). Organization groups are the groups (group
accounts) on this domain; use an internal domain with no MX records, not a
customer's domain. Unset or empty (the default) turns the feature off and
leaves Stalwart's behaviour unchanged.

```json
{"organizationQuotaDomain": "orgs.internal"}
```

The control plane keeps one group per organization on that domain, with the
organization's mailboxes as members, and gives the members quotas that add
up to the organization's limit.

### Behaviour

- **When:** only when a write fails the account's own disk quota check, and
  the account is a member of a group on the organization domain. Members
  with no disk quota (unlimited) are never donors.
- **From whom:** the member with the most free space (quota minus used),
  ties going to the lowest account id.
- **How much:** the largest of the item's size, the account's shortfall
  (more than the item when the account is already over quota) and 50 MB
  (`ORG_QUOTA_MIN_TRANSFER`). The donor always keeps 10 MB free
  (`ORG_QUOTA_DONOR_RESERVE`); a donor that cannot give the whole amount
  gives what it can above its reserve, as long as that covers the
  shortfall.
- **Atomic:** both accounts are read from the registry and both quotas are
  written in one transaction that asserts both objects are unchanged
  (`RegistryStore::write_conditional`, all assertions before any write), so
  two servers cannot hand out the same space. On a conflict the transfer is
  tried once more; a second conflict gives up.
- **No room:** the usual `LimitEvent::Quota` error. Local delivery turns it
  into a temporary failure ("Mailbox over quota"), so the message waits in
  the queue and is retried.
- **Other servers:** after a transfer both accounts go through
  `CacheInvalidationBuilder::process_update` and `Server::invalidate_caches`,
  which clears the local caches and broadcasts `CacheInvalidate` to the
  cluster, the same path an administrator's quota change takes.
- **Logging:** each transfer emits `store.quota-transfer` (info) with the
  account, the donor (`id`), the amount (`size`) and the new limit.

### Where it hooks in

- `Server::has_available_quota` (`crates/common/src/storage/quota.rs`):
  on failure it calls `transfer_organization_quota` before returning the
  error. The Enterprise tenant check after it is untouched. Write paths that
  call it get the transfer: DAV cross-account COPY/MOVE of calendars,
  address books and files, DAV dead properties, IMAP and JMAP metadata,
  calendar scheduling notifications (`groupware/src/calendar/storage.rs`)
  and Exchange virtual folders.
- **Mail ingestion** (`crates/email/src/message/ingest.rs`). In v1.2.0 mail
  delivery, IMAP APPEND, JMAP Email/import and Email/set, Sieve `fileinto`
  and restores never call `has_available_quota`: the account's quota is a
  bound checked inside the write transaction, which cannot move quota. So
  `email_ingest_into` calls `prepare_organization_quota` first: when the
  setting is on and the account (from cached data) is in an organization
  group, it reads the account's usage and, if the message would not fit,
  transfers quota and reloads the account. This is one counter read per
  message for organization members only; nothing for anyone else or with
  the setting off.
- **Not covered:** writes whose only quota check is the in-transaction
  bound or `disk_quota_budget`: IMAP COPY/MOVE and JMAP Email/copy into
  another account, CalDAV/CardDAV/WebDAV PUT, ManageSieve PUTSCRIPT, JMAP
  calendar, contact, file and Sieve writes. They fail with the usual quota
  error; the next delivery to that account moves quota.
- A message appended in a batch (IMAP MULTIAPPEND) is checked on its own,
  not with the rest of the batch, so a batch can still fail at commit when
  the transfer was too small for all of it.

## Mirrored blob store

A blob store type that holds a primary and a secondary blob store and keeps
every blob in both.

| Operation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Write | Write both at once. Succeed when both succeed, or when one succeeds and the other fails or takes longer than `writeTimeout`; record the missed copy in the repair list |
| Both writes fail | Return the error, so mail is refused with a temporary error and the sender retries |
| Read | Read the primary; on an error, a timeout (`readTimeout`) or "not found", read the secondary |
| Delete | Delete from both; a side that fails or times out is recorded in the repair list |
| Store keeps failing | After `failureThreshold` failures in a row, a side is marked down for `downDuration`: reads go to the other store and writes skip it and are recorded. After the pause it is tried again; a failure then marks it down at once |
| Repair | A background task copies or deletes what each side missed |

### Setting

The `BlobStore` settings object, like the other blob store types:

```json
{
  "@type": "Mirrored",
  "primary":   {"@type": "S3", "bucket": "mail-a", "region": {"@type": "Custom", "url": "https://s3.example.com", "region": "eu-central"}},
  "secondary": {"@type": "S3", "bucket": "mail-b", "region": {"@type": "Custom", "url": "https://b2.example.com", "region": "eu-central"}},
  "readTimeout": 2000,
  "writeTimeout": 5000,
  "failureThreshold": 3,
  "downDuration": 30000,
  "repairInterval": 60000
}
```

Durations are in milliseconds; the values shown are the defaults. Each side
can be any blob store type except `Mirrored` (and Enterprise's `Sharded`):
`Default` (the data store), `S3`, `Azure`, `FileSystem`, `FoundationDb`,
`PostgreSql`, `MySql`. The two sides must differ. Choosing any other blob
store type leaves Stalwart's behaviour unchanged.

### Details and decisions

- The mirror works on stored bytes: compression and encryption happen once,
  before the blob is copied to both sides.
- **Repair list:** in the cluster's data store, so every server shares it:
  subspace `System`, keys `0x57 0x01 <side> <blob key>` (Stalwart uses
  `0x00` and `0x01` as first bytes there), value: the operation missed
  (`put` or `delete`) and when. Recording a missed copy is part of the
  write: if it cannot be recorded, the write fails rather than leave a single
  copy nobody knows about.
- **Repair:** blobs are named by their content, so the side that missed
  something is made to match the other side: copy the blob if the other
  side has it, delete it if not. When both sides missed something for the
  same blob, the most recent operation decides. Entries are removed only if
  unchanged since they were read; while either side is marked down the
  entries wait.
- **One server at a time:** every server runs the loop
  (`crates/services/src/blob_mirror.rs`) every `repairInterval`; a lease in
  the in-memory store (the mechanism Stalwart's task manager uses, prefix
  `KV_LOCK_BLOB_MIRROR` = 87) lets only one of them repair, in batches of
  500 entries, renewing the lease between batches.
- **Logging:** `store.blob-mirror-down` (warning) when a side is marked
  down, `store.blob-mirror-up` when it works again, and
  `store.blob-mirror-repair` (info, `total` = repair list length,
  `size` = entries repaired) after each repair run that found entries.
  These are events, not Prometheus metrics: alert on them through a
  tracer or webhook.
- A first read whose side is the only one up has no timeout, and the
  fallback read has none either: they rely on the backend's own timeouts.
  Large blobs on a slow primary can exceed `readTimeout`; they are then read
  from the secondary, and repeated timeouts mark the primary down. Raise
  `readTimeout` if that happens.
- A configuration reload builds a new mirror, so the down/up state starts
  afresh.
- **Not built:** the nightly comparison of both bucket listings (optional in
  the design). Blobs lost outside Stalwart (deleted directly from a bucket)
  are found only by that comparison.

## Account fence

Row-level security in a PostgreSQL data store that shows a signed-in session only the rows of the accounts it may
open, so a bug that reads another account's key gets nothing back. It is per account, finer than per organization.

### Setting

`accountFence` on the `PostgreSql` data store (the data store's file, `config.json`), off by default:

```json
{"@type": "PostgreSql", "host": "db", "database": "stalwart", "authUsername": "stalwart", "accountFence": true}
```

With it on, Stalwart must connect as a role that is not a superuser and has `NOBYPASSRLS`; it refuses to start
otherwise, since such a role would see every row anyway. The role still owns the tables, which is why the policies are
`FORCE`d. Turning the setting off takes the policies off at the next start.

### Behaviour

- **Scope.** Each task runs in a scope (`crates/store/src/fence.rs`): `System` (every row) or a list of accounts. A
  session's scope is set when it authenticates, from its access token: its own account, its groups and the accounts
  shared with it (`AccessToken::all_ids`). A token that may impersonate (an administrator) is `System`. The scope
  comes from who signed in, never from the key being read.
- **Where.** HTTP (JMAP, WebDAV and the rest) gives each request a slot, filled by `authenticate_headers`; IMAP, POP3
  and ManageSieve give each connection one, filled at sign-in (ManageSieve's UNAUTHENTICATE empties it). IMAP
  commands and JMAP's WebSocket, which run as spawned tasks, take the session's scope with them. Everything else runs
  as `System`: delivery, the queue, indexing, reports, housekeeping, SMTP sessions (including the one JMAP's
  EmailSubmission spawns, which only queues), and any task spawned without `inherit`.
- **On the connection.** `PostgresStore::conn()` checks a connection out of the pool and, when the connection's
  scope differs from the task's, sets `stalwart.scope`, `stalwart.accounts` (hex key prefixes) and
  `stalwart.account_ids` (decimal, for `accid`) with one statement before handing it over. A new connection has no
  scope, and a connection with none sees nothing, so a failure fails closed. Each connection remembers its scope, so a
  run of work in one scope costs nothing more; `Clean` pool recycling resets it.
- **Tables** (`crates/store/src/backend/postgres/fence.rs`), by how their keys start:

  | Table | Rule |
  |---|---|
  | `p` properties and metadata, `c` immutable, `i` indexes, `v` index properties (account id first) | fenced |
  | `l` change logs (account id first) | fenced, except share notifications (collection 9): sharing writes them into the account it shares with |
  | `n` counters (kind byte, then account id) | document ids, object counts, quotas and mailbox UIDs fenced by account; change ids open (numbers only; sharing bumps the other account's); other kinds open |
  | `a` ACLs (grantee, then owner) | either in scope |
  | `s_email`, `s_cal`, `s_card`, `s_file` (`accid`) | fenced |
  | all others: directory, registry, queue, blobs and blob links, reports, telemetry, tasks, spam samples, archived items, the search queue, `s_trace` | open: not per account, and read by sessions |

  Each policy is `stalwart_fence_system() OR <rule>`, for reading and writing; the scope functions are called as
  `(SELECT f())`, once per query.

### Tested and measured (26 November 2026, macOS, PostgreSQL 17, release build)

An end-to-end script, kept with the fork's tests outside this branch, drove a running build with the fence on and
passed all 16 checks: own mail over JMAP and IMAP (APPEND, then FETCH), another account refused, a mailbox shared
over JMAP and read by the account it was shared with (its scope then holds both accounts), the scopes Stalwart sent
(one account per person, two for the share), and in the database itself a person's scope seeing their rows and not
another's, `system` seeing all and an unscoped connection nothing. Starting with a superuser and the fence on is
refused. The fork's other tests pass, and with the setting off the policies come off at start.

Cost, on a laptop against a local PostgreSQL:

- PostgreSQL alone: reading one account's 505 rows by key range, 0.23 ms as `system` and 0.46 ms in the account's
  scope (about 0.45 µs a row); a point read about 14 µs more per statement, mostly the scope functions.
- Through JMAP: `Email/query` and `Email/get` of 500 messages, median 4.0–4.7 ms with the fence and 2.2–3.9 ms
  without, one person or two taking turns (a scope change on most checkouts). Noisy at this size; measure on the
  production database before relying on it.

Debug builds need a bigger worker stack (`RUST_MIN_STACK=16777216`): a JMAP request overflowed tokio's default 2 MB,
release builds don't.

### Known limits

- Delivery runs as `System`, so it isn't fenced; there isolation rests on the directory and on whoever provisions it.
- A session's scope is fixed at sign-in (IMAP, POP3, ManageSieve) or per request (HTTP). A share made after an IMAP
  session signed in isn't in its scope until it signs in again, so that shared folder fails to open until then.
- Writes a session makes into an account outside its scope, other than the two exceptions above, fail. Calendar
  scheduling between local accounts (iTIP delivered in the organizer's request) is the one known case.
- Not fenced in memory: caches, and the bus, are Stalwart's own.
